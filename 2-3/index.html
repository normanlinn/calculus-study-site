Calculating Limits Using the Limit Laws

Plug in first. If you get 0/0: factor, use a conjugate, or combine fractions. Plus the Squeeze Theorem and absolute values.

0 / 8 problems mastered

Midterm 1 material, back on the final. The final is comprehensive, so these limits show up again, usually as 2–4 short problems worth about 15% of the test.

Step 1: plug in

If the function is defined at aaa (polynomials, roots, trig, …), the limit is just f(a)f(a)f(a).

0/0: simplify

Factor and cancel · multiply by the conjugate (square roots) · combine fractions (fractions inside fractions). Then plug in again.

Traps

Writing lim on every line

Keep writing lim⁡x→a\lim_{x\to a}limx→a​ until the step where you actually plug in. Dropping it early costs points.

Cancel then plug

After cancelling, you must still substitute aaa. Stopping at x−2x-2x−2 isn't an answer; −5-5−5 is.
