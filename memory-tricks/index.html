§3.6 Logs and exponentials: one idea, two directions

The ln rule
(ln⁡u)′=u′u(\ln u)'=\frac{u'}{u}(lnu)′=uu′​

“Derivative of the inside over the inside.” Works for ln⁡∣u∣\ln|u|ln∣u∣ too.

Other bases
(log⁡bx)′=1xln⁡b(\log_b x)'=\frac{1}{x\ln b}(logb​x)′=xlnb1​
(bx)′=bxln⁡b(b^x)'=b^x\ln b(bx)′=bxlnb

“Logs divide by ln b, powers multiply by ln b.” When b=eb=eb=e, ln⁡e=1\ln e=1lne=1 and it disappears.

Log laws first

Before differentiating ln⁡(product, quotient or power)\ln(\text{product, quotient or power})ln(product, quotient or power), split it: ln⁡(ab)=ln⁡a+ln⁡b\ln(ab)=\ln a+\ln bln(ab)=lna+lnb, ln⁡ab=ln⁡a−ln⁡b\ln\frac ab=\ln a-\ln blnba​=lna−lnb, ln⁡an=nln⁡a\ln a^n=n\ln alnan=nlna. Then each piece is easy.

xxx^xxx, xsin⁡xx^{\sin x}xsinx, xln⁡xx^{\ln x}xlnx: take ln of both sides first (log differentiation), then y′y=…\frac{y'}{y}=\dotsyy′​=…, then multiply by yyy.

§3.9 Related rates: the 4-step routine

The routine

Draw → Equation → Differentiate → Plug in. Say it as “D-E-D-P”. Numbers for “this moment” go in last, after differentiating with respect to ttt.

Shapes to know

Sphere V=43πr3V=\frac43\pi r^3V=34​πr3, S=4πr2S=4\pi r^2S=4πr2. Cone V=13πr2hV=\frac13\pi r^2hV=31​πr2h. Cylinder V=πr2hV=\pi r^2hV=πr2h. Circle A=πr2A=\pi r^2A=πr2. Right triangle x2+y2=z2x^2+y^2=z^2x2+y2=z2.

Cone trick

Water in a cone: use similar triangles (rh=RH\frac rh=\frac{R}{H}hr​=HR​) to replace rrr with a multiple of hhh before differentiating.

Signs

Something shrinking or leaking has a negative rate. Constants (a fixed pole, a fixed altitude) can be plugged in early; changing things cannot.

§3.10 Linear approximation: it's just the tangent line

Linearization
L(x)=f(a)+f′(a)(x−a)L(x)=f(a)+f'(a)(x-a)L(x)=f(a)+f′(a)(x−a)

“Start at the height, move with the slope.” Pick aaa close to your number where f(a)f(a)f(a) is easy: 4.02→a=4\sqrt{4.02}\to a=44.02​→a=4, e0.1→a=0e^{0.1}\to a=0e0.1→a=0.

Differential
dy=f′(x) dxdy=f'(x)\,dxdy=f′(x)dx

“Derivative, then attach dx.”

Δy vs dy

Δy=f(x+Δx)−f(x)\Delta y=f(x+\Delta x)-f(x)Δy=f(x+Δx)−f(x) is the real change (on the curve). dydydy is the predicted change (on the tangent line).

§3.11 Hyperbolic: the trig table with the minus moved

Definitions
sinh⁡x=ex−e−x2\sinh x=\frac{e^x-e^{-x}}{2}sinhx=2ex−e−x​
cosh⁡x=ex+e−x2\cosh x=\frac{e^x+e^{-x}}{2}coshx=2ex+e−x​

“sinh is minus, cosh is plus.” (s and m sit together; c and p are both round.) At 0: sinh⁡0=0\sinh0=0sinh0=0, cosh⁡0=1\cosh0=1cosh0=1, like sin and cos.

Derivatives
(sinh⁡x)′=cosh⁡x(\sinh x)'=\cosh x(sinhx)′=coshx
(cosh⁡x)′=sinh⁡x(\cosh x)'=\sinh x(coshx)′=sinhx
(tanh⁡x)′=sech⁡2x(\tanh x)'=\operatorname{sech}^2x(tanhx)′=sech2x

Just like trig, except cosh has no minus.

Where the minus went
(coth⁡x)′=−csch⁡2x(\coth x)'=-\operatorname{csch}^2x(cothx)′=−csch2x
(sech⁡x)′=−sech⁡xtanh⁡x(\operatorname{sech}x)'=-\operatorname{sech}x\tanh x(sechx)′=−sechxtanhx
(csch⁡x)′=−csch⁡xcoth⁡x(\operatorname{csch}x)'=-\operatorname{csch}x\coth x(cschx)′=−cschxcothx

“The minus moves from cosh to sech.” Every other sign matches trig.

Identity
cosh⁡2x−sinh⁡2x=1\cosh^2x-\sinh^2x=1cosh2x−sinh2x=1

Trig has a plus; hyperbolic has a minus. Use it like sin⁡2+cos⁡2=1\sin^2+\cos^2=1sin2+cos2=1 to find sinh from cosh.

§4.1 Max and min: the checklist

Critical number

A number ccc in the domain where f′(c)=0f'(c)=0f′(c)=0 or f′(c)f'(c)f′(c) doesn't exist. Find f′f'f′, factor it, set the top = 0 and check where the bottom = 0.

Closed Interval Method

“Critical numbers inside + both endpoints → table → biggest and smallest.” Evaluate the original fff, not f′f'f′.

Extreme Value Theorem

Continuous on a closed interval [a,b][a,b][a,b] ⇒ an absolute max and min both exist. Open or half-open intervals might have none.

Fermat's Theorem

Local max or min at ccc and f′(c)f'(c)f′(c) exists ⇒ f′(c)=0f'(c)=0f′(c)=0. The reverse is false: f(x)=x3f(x)=x^3f(x)=x3 has f′(0)=0f'(0)=0f′(0)=0 but no max or min there.

Squaring the inside wrong

(ex)2=e2x(e^x)^2=e^{2x}(ex)2=e2x, not ex2e^{x^2}ex2. (3x)2=9x2(3x)^2=9x^2(3x)2=9x2, not 3x23x^23x2. Square the whole inside.

Forgetting u′ in inverse trig

(tan⁡−1x2)′=2x1+x4(\tan^{-1}x^2)'=\frac{2x}{1+x^4}(tan−1x2)′=1+x42x​. The 2x2x2x on top is the chain rule; it's easy to forget.

Plugging in before differentiating

In related rates, a changing quantity replaced by a number has derivative 0, so its rate disappears and the answer is wrong.

Counting a point outside the domain

If fff itself is undefined at ccc, then ccc is not a critical number, even though f′f'f′ is undefined there too.

Skipping the endpoints

On [a,b][a,b][a,b] the absolute max is often at an endpoint. Always put f(a)f(a)f(a) and f(b)f(b)f(b) in your table.

Quiz 3 · Trig derivatives: learn 3, get 6

Don't memorize six separate facts. Learn the three “plain” ones, then use one rule to get the other three for free.

Step 1. Learn these three:

FunctionDerivative

Step 2. The “co-” rule: put co in front of every function, then add a minus sign.

PlainAdd “co” + minus
(sin⁡x)′=cos⁡x(\sin x)'=\cos x(sinx)′=cosx(cos⁡x)′=−sin⁡x(\cos x)'=-\sin x(cosx)′=−sinx
(tan⁡x)′=sec⁡2x(\tan x)'=\sec^2x(tanx)′=sec2x(cot⁡x)′=−csc⁡2x(\cot x)'=-\csc^2x(cotx)′=−csc2x
(sec⁡x)′=sec⁡xtan⁡x(\sec x)'=\sec x\tan x(secx)′=secxtanx(csc⁡x)′=−csc⁡xcot⁡x(\csc x)'=-\csc x\cot x(cscx)′=−cscxcotx

Adding “co” to cos gives “co-cos”, which is just sin. So in the first row, “cos x” becomes “sin x” and picks up the minus: (cos⁡x)′=−sin⁡x(\cos x)'=-\sin x(cosx)′=−sinx.

The one sentence

“Co” functions get a minus. cos, cot and csc have negative derivatives. sin, tan and sec don't.

Partners

tan goes with sec, cot goes with csc. tan⁡→sec⁡2\tan\to\sec^2tan→sec2, sec⁡→sec⁡tan⁡\sec\to\sec\tansec→sectan. cot⁡→−csc⁡2\cot\to-\csc^2cot→−csc2, csc⁡→−csc⁡cot⁡\csc\to-\csc\cotcsc→−csccot.

Keep yourself

sec and csc keep themselves: sec⁡→sec⁡⋅tan⁡\sec\to\boldsymbol{\sec}\cdot\tansec→sec⋅tan, csc⁡→−csc⁡⋅cot⁡\csc\to-\boldsymbol{\csc}\cdot\cotcsc→−csc⋅cot.

The circle

sin and cos cycle every 4 derivatives: sin⁡→cos⁡→−sin⁡→−cos⁡→sin⁡\sin\to\cos\to-\sin\to-\cos\to\sinsin→cos→−sin→−cos→sin. One quarter turn per derivative.

Blanked on the exam? Rebuild tan in 20 seconds

You only need sin, cos and the quotient rule. Write tan⁡x=sin⁡xcos⁡x\tan x=\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}tanx=cosxsinx​:

  1. Low d-high minus high d-low: cos⁡x⋅cos⁡x−sin⁡x⋅(−sin⁡x)cos⁡2x\dfrac{\cos x\cdot\cos x-\sin x\cdot(-\sin x)}{\cos^2x}cos2xcosx⋅cosx−sinx⋅(−sinx)​
  2. Top: cos⁡2x+sin⁡2x=1\cos^2x+\sin^2x=1cos2x+sin2x=1.
  3. So (tan⁡x)′=1cos⁡2x=sec⁡2x(\tan x)'=\dfrac{1}{\cos^2x}=\sec^2x(tanx)′=cos2x1​=sec2x.
  4. Same idea for sec: sec⁡x=1cos⁡x\sec x=\dfrac1{\cos x}secx=cosx1​ gives sin⁡xcos⁡2x=sec⁡xtan⁡x\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos^2x}=\sec x\tan xcos2xsinx​=secxtanx.

How to make it stick

  1. Blank-page recall. Once a day, write every formula from memory on an empty sheet. Then check it against the cheat sheet. Recalling works much better than rereading.
  2. Say the phrases out loud. They're written to be spoken. Rhythm helps memory.
  3. Drill both directions. Use the flashcards above both ways: “tan⁡x→\tan x\totanx→ ?” and “sec⁡2x\sec^2xsec2x is the derivative of ?”.
  4. Short and spread out. Ten minutes a day for several days beats one long night. Mix in a few problems from §3.3 and the Quick quiz.
  5. When stuck, derive it. Rebuilding tan from sin/cos also practices the quotient rule.
