Implicit Differentiation

Finding slopes on curves you can't (or don't want to) solve for y.

0 / 10 problems mastered

A curve like x3+y3=6xyx^3+y^3=6xyx3+y3=6xy isn't a function y=f(x)y=f(x)y=f(x), but near most points it looks like one. So treat yyy as an unknown function of xxx and differentiate both sides with respect to xxx.

The one rule: every time you differentiate something with yyy in it, the chain rule attaches a factor of y′y'y′. So ddx(y3)=3y2 y′\frac{d}{dx}(y^3)=3y^2\,y'dxd​(y3)=3y2y′, ddx(sin⁡y)=cos⁡y y′\frac{d}{dx}(\sin y)=\cos y\,y'dxd​(siny)=cosyy′, and ddx(xy)=y+x y′\frac{d}{dx}(xy)=y+x\,y'dxd​(xy)=y+xy′ (product rule).

Recipe:

  1. Differentiate both sides with respect to xxx. Attach y′y'y′ to every yyy-term.
  2. Move all y′y'y′ terms to one side, everything else to the other.
  3. Factor out y′y'y′ and divide.

Traps

Forgetting y′ on y-terms

ddxy2=2y y′\frac{d}{dx}y^2=2y\,y'dxd​y2=2yy′, not 2y2y2y. This is the #1 mistake.

xy is a product

ddx(xy)=y+xy′\frac{d}{dx}(xy)=y+xy'dxd​(xy)=y+xy′. Don't write just y′y'y′ or 1⋅y′1\cdot y'1⋅y′.

Constant on the right

The derivative of the 25 in x2+y2=25x^2+y^2=25x2+y2=25 is 0. Don't drop the whole right side when it has variables.
